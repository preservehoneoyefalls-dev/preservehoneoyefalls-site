The proposal

What is Copperfield?

Morrell Team In Motion LLC proposes a large subdivision on about 179 acres of farmland east of North Main Street (Route 65) and north of East Street, inside the Village.

412homes, townhomes and apartments
1,000+new residents, according to the developer's own engineer
47%Village growth since 2020 (about 1,285 people), counting Wolfsberger Park next door
2027–42built in 7 phases over about 15 years
~300more car trips in the evening rush hour, by the developer's own traffic study

The plans include an apartment building up to 52 feet tall and homes with 5-foot side yards and more lot coverage than Village zoning normally allows.

New streets would connect at Hyde Park (off North Main Street), Chase Meadow Trail, Fairlawn Drive and Quaker Meeting House Road. Hyde Park would change from a one-way street to two-way.

Why 47%? Wolfsberger Park, built by the same builder off Route 65 since the 2020 Census, adds another 118 homes, or about 270 people at the Village's average household size. Add Copperfield's 1,000+ and the Village would gain about 1,285 residents, roughly 47% more than its 2020 population of 2,706.

Why it matters

Questions neighbors are asking

We're not against every new home. In the Village's own survey, residents named the "small town/village atmosphere" as their top reason for living here. We're asking that a project this size be studied fully and fit that village.

Traffic on Route 65 and East Street

Most Copperfield traffic would use North Main Street, a state road, and a new connection would send more onto East Street through Fairlawn Drive. East Street already backs up at school drop-off and pick-up; the Fire Department has asked for relief there. The developer's study counted one weekday, assumed no other new development, and left out the Main, East and Monroe intersection. It recommends no road improvements.

Who pays

No study shows what Copperfield would cost the Village: new streets and utilities to maintain, about 81 acres of open space handed to the Village, and more demand on fire and EMS. Nothing in the plans has the developer sharing those costs. We think it should.

Stormwater and flooding

The site's clay soils absorb almost no water, and the developer's own environmental form says runoff will reach neighboring properties. Neighbors downstream already report flooding.

A full environmental review

A project this size calls for a full Environmental Impact Statement that looks at all phases together, along with Wolfsberger Park and other planned development, before any decision is made.

What comes next

We worry that a development this size at the edge of the Village, with 1,000+ new residents and hundreds more cars at rush hour, will invite drive-throughs, franchises and other unwanted development.

About us

Who we are

Preserve Honeoye Falls is a nonpartisan group of Village of Honeoye Falls and Town of Mendon residents. We came together in 2026 when the Copperfield proposal reached the Zoning Board of Appeals.

We have filed to incorporate as a New York not-for-profit so we can raise and manage money responsibly and work with experienced land-use counsel. Everything we share comes from public documents: the developer's own application, letters from state and local agencies, official meeting minutes, and the Village's Comprehensive Plan.
